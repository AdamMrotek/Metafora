The patient portal

A call that writes itself down while you have it.

This is the other half of the clinician portal. A patient opens a link, sees who is asking, and talks — one continuous conversation, not a form read aloud. There is nothing to press between answers and nothing to hold down: the line is open, they talk when they have something to say, and they can interrupt whenever they like.

One tap to start, none after Named clinician at the top of it Interrupt it whenever you like
Design language: unchanged. Same tokens, same turquoise, same rule that anything coloured means something. What changes is density — the clinician screen is scanned, this one is spoken to, so the type is larger, the targets are bigger, and there is nothing on it that counts, times, or paginates a conversation.

The screen

Three quarters conversation, one quarter what you have said

It answers three questions, in this order:

  • Who is asking me this, and about what?
  • When do I talk?
  • Did that count?

The first is the header, and it is a person's name. The second answers itself: whenever you like — the line is open, and the bar under the thread says so rather than asking for a tap. The third is the notes card in the top right corner, which fills in as the call runs and never asks for anything.

Goal: it feels like being asked, not like being processed — and a patient never wonders whether something they said was heard.

The call

One tap to start, and none after it

Push-to-talk is the wrong model for this. Holding a button while you describe a symptom means thinking about the button, and the first time someone lets go mid-sentence and loses half an answer, they stop saying the difficult part. So the line opens once and stays open: the patient talks, stops, thinks, starts again, and interrupts — the same things they would do on the phone to the practice.

The bar below the thread reports which of those is happening. It is a status, not a control, and it never moves.

How it ends

The line closes on a sentence, not a submit

When there is nothing left to ask, the assistant says so out loud, the call bar goes quiet, and the notes card stays exactly where it is — a patient's last view of this product should be the list of what they said, not an empty thread and a spinner. Nothing is submitted at the end, because everything was saved as it was said.

From there the interview appears in the clinician's Review ready queue, where a named clinician reads it, edits the summary, and signs it. That screen is documented in ux-clinical-dashboard.html, and the two pages share one token file on purpose.

The handover: everything in the notes card is a field on the clinician's review composer. A patient watching that list fill in is watching the clinical record being written, which is why it is worth a quarter of the screen.
