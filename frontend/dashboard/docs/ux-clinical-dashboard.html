How it works

The five steps, and the three ways it ends

The questions are fixed and approved long before any of this: nobody writes an interview per patient. What varies is how far it gets, and every interview ends in one of the three ways below.

STEP 1Deploy A clinician sends a structured interview to a patient on their panel. seconds · deployment dashboard
STEP 2Intake The patient answers by voice or text, in their own time, in their own words. minutes · patient phone or web
STEP 3Return The transcript and recording are saved to the patient’s record: ready to review, or needing you sooner. instant · automatic
STEP 4Review The clinician reads the evidence and the machine-drafted summary. minutes · patient profile
STEP 5Sign A named registrant commits to a clinical impression, pinned to the record's hash. irreversible · patient profile
Rare · interrupts Urgent escalation Needs a reviewer’s immediate attention. The agent escalates, the interview stops, the patient is told a clinician will contact them, and a named clinician is alerted. Urgent review 06:41
Routine · interrupts Issue raised The interview did not complete, or the agent raised a soft escalation concern. issue raisedabandoned
The common case Review ready It ran to the end and the output is complete. Most interviews end here. review ready

The dashboard

What needs you, in the order it needs you

It answers three questions, and it asks them in this order:

  • Is anything on fire?
  • What do I owe a decision on?
  • Who is on my list?

The order they are asked in is the layout.

Goal: you open this screen once, and you know what to do next before you have clicked anything.

Deployments

Four choices, and then it goes out

Sending an interview is one decision made four times: who, what, when, how. A patient, a call type, a slot, and how the call reaches them. Everything else, the wording, the version and the escalation set, is settled before this screen and is not a clinician's choice.

One patient at a time. There is no batch, no cohort and no list upload, because the moment a send covers twelve people it stops being a clinical decision and becomes an administrative one.

Goal: sending an interview takes four decisions and no training, and every call you have booked stays in front of you until it goes out.
